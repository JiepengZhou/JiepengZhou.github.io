# MIND: From Passive Mimicry to Active Reasoning through Capability-Aware Multi-Perspective CoT Distillation

---

## MOTIVATION

- **Distribution collapse under single-path supervision.** Conventional knowledge distillation typically uses a single optimal reasoning trajectory generated by a large teacher model as the supervision target. However, LLMs can reason through multiple paradigms, including formal symbolic reasoning, intuition, and counterfactual analysis. Forcing a compact student model to imitate only one trajectory suppresses this strategic diversity and weakens out-of-distribution (OOD) generalization.
- **Limited exploitation of multiple reasoning paths.** Existing multi-path distillation methods generally aggregate candidate trajectories through voting or score-based weighting while assuming that the paths are independent. Consequently, they fail to model the complementary information shared across reasoning perspectives.
- **Capability mismatch between teacher and student.** A trajectory considered optimal by a large teacher model may lie outside the effective learning distribution of a smaller student model. Such supervision can introduce noisy gradients and increase the risk of reasoning hallucinations.
- **Evolving student capability.** A student's learning preference changes throughout training. It may initially benefit from explicit step-by-step derivations but later become capable of learning more abstract reasoning patterns. A static distillation strategy cannot accommodate this progression and may lead to inefficient optimization or catastrophic forgetting.

---

## METHODOLOGY

### 1. Multi-Perspective Dataset Construction

We define eight reasoning paradigms and design a dedicated prompt for each paradigm. Given the same question, the teacher model generates eight distinct reasoning trajectories and their corresponding answers. A judge model removes trajectories with incorrect answers, while easy examples are downsampled and difficult examples are retained. This procedure produces a compact training set of only 497 questions, each paired with eight chain-of-thought (CoT) trajectories.

| Reasoning perspective | Description |
|---|---|
| `Formal Symbolic` | Performs rigorous symbolic derivations without intuitive shortcuts. |
| `Intuitive` | Uses practical heuristics and experiential analogies while minimizing formal notation. |
| `Decomposition` | Divides the problem into numbered subproblems and solves them sequentially. |
| `Planning` | Establishes a high-level solution plan before executing individual steps. |
| `Analogy` | Maps the original problem to a simpler structurally similar example. |
| `Socratic` | Progressively derives the solution through self-questioning and self-explanation. |
| `Contrastive` | Compares and eliminates multiple candidate solutions to identify the most plausible one. |
| `Counterfactual` | Modifies problem conditions and reasons backward to identify critical constraints. |

![t-SNE]({{ site.baseurl }}/images/Papers/MIND/t-SNE.png){: width="60%" }

The t-SNE visualization shows that the eight perspectives form well-separated clusters in the representation space. This suggests that they differ not merely in linguistic expression but also in their underlying activation patterns.

- Before the main distillation stage, we train eight specialized student models, each using data from only one reasoning perspective.
- We extract last-layer hidden representations from these models, model their distribution using Sentence-BERT and a Dirichlet Process Mixture Model (DPMM), and visualize the resulting representations with t-SNE.

### 2. MetaNet

- MetaNet takes a question and eight candidate trajectories $r_k$ as input and predicts a compatibility score $s_k$ between each trajectory and the student's current capability.
- Multi-head self-attention aggregates features across all eight perspectives, explicitly modeling complementary and collaborative relationships among reasoning trajectories.
- Rather than relying on noisy single-step losses, MetaNet accumulates the student's long-term learning preferences. Multiple MLP scoring heads produce the score matrix, reducing weight oscillation caused by transient loss spikes.

### 3. Training Procedure

#### 3.1 Feedback-Driven Inertia Calibration

The central objective is to align MetaNet's compatibility estimates with the student's evolving capability by using long-term loss signals instead of instantaneous per-step feedback.

**Stage-wise training strategy:**

- **Warm-up stage:** MetaNet is initialized over a small number of epochs.
- **Joint-training stage:** MetaNet and the student are optimized jointly. MetaNet uses a smaller learning rate and larger gradient accumulation window, introducing optimization inertia that filters stochastic noise from individual mini-batches.

MetaNet is optimized using a ListNet-KL ranking objective:

$$\mathcal{L}_{meta}=D_{KL}\big(\pi_{\tau}(s)\,\|\,\pi_{\tau}(-\mathcal{L}_{real})\big)$$

- $\mathcal{L}_{real}$ denotes the student's cross-entropy loss on trajectory $r_k$. A higher loss indicates lower compatibility with the student's current capability.
- $\pi_\tau(\cdot)$ denotes a Softmax distribution with temperature $\tau$.
- The objective minimizes the KL divergence between MetaNet's predicted score distribution $\pi_\tau(s)$ and the empirical learnability distribution $\pi_\tau(-\mathcal{L}_{real})$.

#### 3.2 Dynamic Selection of Compatible Trajectories

MetaNet dynamically retains trajectories that are compatible with the student and filters out noisy or excessively difficult supervision:

$$\mathcal{I}_{dyn}=\left\{k\mid s_k\geq \max(s)\cdot\beta\right\}$$

where $\beta$ is a threshold hyperparameter. Only trajectories whose scores reach at least $\beta$ times the maximum score are retained.

The weights are normalized within the selected subset:

$$\alpha_k=
\frac{\exp\left(s_k/\tau_{student}\right)}
{\sum_{j\in\mathcal{I}_{dyn}}\exp\left(s_j/\tau_{student}\right)},
\quad \forall k\in\mathcal{I}_{dyn}$$

#### 3.3 Student Objective

The overall student objective combines preference-weighted supervised fine-tuning with consistency regularization:

$$\boldsymbol{\mathcal{L}_{total}=\mathcal{L}_{SFT}+\lambda\mathcal{L}_{cons}}$$

**Preference-weighted SFT loss:**

$$\mathcal{L}_{SFT}=
\sum_{k\in\mathcal{I}_{dyn}}
\alpha_k\cdot\mathcal{L}_{CE}\left(r_k\mid x;\theta\right)$$

The compatibility weights $\alpha_k$ encourage the student to prioritize trajectories that match its current learning capability.

**Pairwise consistency regularization:**

$$\mathcal{L}_{cons}=
\sum_{\substack{i,j\in\mathcal{I}_{dyn}\\i<j}}
(\alpha_i\cdot\alpha_j)\cdot JSD(P_i\|P_j)$$

- $P_i$ and $P_j$ are the answer distributions induced by two different trajectories for the same question.
- Jensen-Shannon divergence measures the discrepancy between these distributions.
- The regularizer encourages answer-level agreement across perspectives, preventing contradictory reasoning patterns and fragmented representations.

### 4. Experimental Setup

- **Student models:** Qwen2.5-1.5B, Qwen2.5-7B, and Llama3.1-8B.
- **In-distribution mathematical reasoning:** MATH500, GSM8K, and SVAMP.
- **OOD generalization:** CSQA for commonsense question answering, StrategyQA for implicit multi-step reasoning, and GPQA-Diamond for graduate-level scientific reasoning.
- **Baselines:** zero-shot CoT, SbS-KD, MCC-KD, MoDE-CoTD, EDIT, and the ablation variant MIND without dynamic fusion.

## RESULTS

![Main results]({{ site.baseurl }}/images/Papers/MIND/result-main.png)
![Ablation study 1]({{ site.baseurl }}/images/Papers/MIND/result-ablation-1.png)
![Ablation study 2]({{ site.baseurl }}/images/Papers/MIND/result-ablation-2.png)

- MIND narrows the capability gap between compact students and larger foundation models, outperforming state-of-the-art baselines by an average of **3.27%** on in-distribution benchmarks.
- Whereas conventional multi-path distillation methods often degrade on OOD benchmarks, MIND achieves an average improvement of **7.53%**.
- MIND reaches state-of-the-art performance using only 497 training examples. Its dynamic adaptation mechanism offsets the modest computational overhead introduced by multi-perspective reasoning and MetaNet, resulting in favorable overall training efficiency.
- Ablation studies show that replacing long-term feedback with instantaneous losses or removing MetaNet's self-attention layer substantially degrades performance, confirming the importance of stable capability estimation and cross-path complementarity.
